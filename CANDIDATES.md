# Candidates

Scored out of 25: (1) clearly a one-star review by a house cat about its
human, (2) funny on first read, (3) deadpan shape of a real review,
(4) complaint goes somewhere only a cat would go, (5) single line, under
200 characters, clean and punchy. Each criterion is worth 5 points.

## Champion — 25/25

**1. "One star. He let me see the bottom of my food bowl. The bottom. I am not an animal." — 83 characters — 5/5/5/5/5**

The repeated word does the joke: a half-empty bowl is, to a cat, an empty
bowl, and the grievance is treated as a serious injustice. The closing
denial is ironic because the speaker plainly is an animal, which lands the
deadpan tone. Nothing in it can be cut.

## The other seven

**2. "One star. He dangles a red dot I can never catch, then switches it off and laughs. I have reported him for false advertising." — 125 characters — 24/25 (5/5/5/5/4)**

An unwinnable game presented as a consumer complaint, with the formal
language of a report filed against the human. Loses one point only because
the middle clause carries one beat more than the joke strictly needs.

**3. "One star. He puts a tree inside the house, decorates it, then gets angry when I climb it. It was obviously mine." — 112 characters — 24/25 (5/5/5/4/5)**

A familiar household object is reframed as furniture installed for the
cat, so the scolding becomes the absurd part. Loses one point because
climbing indoor greenery is a well-worn cat joke, so the twist is slightly
less fresh than the champion's.

**4. "One star. He drove me to a stranger who looked in my ears and weighed me. I bit him. I would do it again." — 105 characters — 23/25 (5/4/5/4/5)**

A routine check-up retold as an assault with no remorse, and the flat
confession at the end keeps the review shape. Loses two points: the bite
is a generic animal reaction rather than a uniquely feline grievance, and
the laugh depends on the final sentence alone.

**5. "One star. I performed my best zoomies at 3 a.m. and he slept through all of it. Zero applause, zero encore, zero respect." — 121 characters — 24/25 (5/5/5/5/4)**

A middle-of-the-night sprint is treated as a stage performance given to an
ungrateful audience, and the triple zero count reads like a scoring rubric.
Loses one point because the three-part ending is a touch list-like next to
the champion's tighter finish.

**6. "One star. He closes the bathroom door, then acts surprised when I scream at it like a ghost. I live here too." — 109 characters — 23/25 (5/4/5/5/4)**

A shut door becomes a personal insult, and the wailing is presented as a
reasonable response rather than the problem. Loses two points: the closing
line states the grievance plainly instead of twisting it, and the laugh is
gentler than the top lines.

**7. "One star. After four years together, he put me in water on purpose. I am writing this review from under the bed." — 112 characters — 23/25 (5/4/5/4/5)**

A bath is framed as a betrayal after years of trust, and the hiding place
is the review desk. Loses two points: dislike of water is shared by many
pets, so the twist is less cat-only, and the joke leans on the hiding place
rather than the complaint itself.

**8. "One star. He grew a salad in a pot and then yelled when I ate it. If it was not food, why did it taste like food?" — 113 characters — 24/25 (5/5/5/5/4)**

A potted plant is reclassified as dinner, and the cat's defence is pure
logic applied to the wrong premise. Loses one point because the closing
question softens the deadpan review voice a fraction compared with a flat
statement.
